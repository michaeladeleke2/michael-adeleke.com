Concussion education usually takes the form of a symptom checklist. The
problem with a checklist is that it asks an athlete to recognize, from the
inside, a condition that impairs exactly the faculties needed to recognize it.

Collision Course approaches this differently. It simulates the perceptual and
cognitive effects of concussion, including visual disturbance, delayed
response, and difficulty tracking instructions, then asks the user to complete
ordinary tasks under them. The learning goal is not a list of symptoms but a felt sense
of what impairment is like, and by extension, why the person next to you might
not be able to report it accurately.

## What we measured

The work connected to a parallel line on physiological sensing in VR, using
EmotiBit to look at stress response during immersive experiences. That pairing of a
designed experience with a physiological signal from the person having it is
the through-line to the radar work. Both are about instrumenting an interaction
so that something otherwise invisible becomes legible.

The project took 2nd place at the Morgan TechFest poster competition in 2023
and was presented at the International Conference on Applied Human Factors and
Ergonomics in 2025.
