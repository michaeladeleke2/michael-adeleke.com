## What the activity asked

Plan one class session from start to finish using the bookend, overlay and interleave structure from Barkley and Major's Engaged Teaching, and name the classroom assessment technique sitting behind every point where I check what students have understood.

## What I made

A Week 5 session on loops, in six parts. It opens and closes on the same instrument, a Kahoot run as an individual readiness assessment, and spends the middle alternating between watching me write code and writing their own.

## What I learned

Laying it out this way made the time budget visible, and the time budget is the thing I had been getting wrong. Twenty two of the fifty minutes are students at a keyboard, eighteen are a demonstration with an activity running underneath it, and ten are a whole class check. I only know those numbers because the structure forced me to add them up.

The bookends are the part I would defend. My [debugging lesson plan](/teaching/debugging-lesson-plan/), five weeks earlier, had exactly one individual checkpoint and it landed at minute 46, which meant I learned nothing about any particular student until the session was over. Here the same individual instrument runs at both ends, so there is evidence at minute five and again at minute forty five, and because it is the same instrument twice the two results can be compared rather than just collected.

The overlay is what stops the demonstration blocks from being passive. While I write and trace a loop, students are committing to a prediction in a sentence stem; while I break a program on purpose, they are filling guided notes. Neither block asks them to simply watch.

What I am less sure about is leaning on the same timed multiple choice format at both ends. It makes the comparison clean, but a student who freezes under a countdown is invisible to me at the start of the session and invisible again at the end, and that is the one student this plan is least likely to catch.

## How this shapes my teaching

I want to budget the minutes before I choose the activities, rather than discovering the split after the plan is written. Hands on the keyboard is the number I care about, and in this session it is the largest share for the first time.

I am keeping the overlay structure, because it turns the part of a session I am most naturally drawn to, live demonstration, into something with a task attached. The [Lecture Style Inventory](/teaching/lecture-style-inventory/) says demonstration is where my instincts go, so a structure that makes demonstration do double duty is working with that rather than against it.

The change I would make before running this is to the exit check. Keeping the opening Kahoot for the comparison, but closing on something untimed and written, would still tell me what the class learned without asking a student to prove it against a countdown.
