## What the activity asked

Plan one class session from start to finish using the bookend, overlay and interleave structure from Barkley and Major's Engaged Teaching, and name the classroom assessment technique sitting behind every point where I check what students have understood.

## What I made

A Week 5 session on loops, in six parts. It opens and closes on the same instrument, a Kahoot run as an individual readiness assessment, and spends the middle alternating between watching me write code and writing their own.

## What I learned

I budgeted the minutes before I chose the activities. Twenty two of the fifty are students at a keyboard, eighteen are a demonstration with an activity running underneath it, and ten are a whole class check, and those shares were the decision rather than the result of one. Writing my [debugging lesson plan](/teaching/debugging-lesson-plan/) the other way round, activities first, is how that session ended up weighted towards me talking without my noticing until I added it up afterwards.

The Kahoots are not a structural decision. I use Kahoot because I have always used it and because it is a quick, honest check of whether a room knows something. Drawing the session out showed me a property of that habit I had not chosen on purpose: running the same instrument at both ends makes the two results comparable, so the exit check measures movement rather than just attainment. It also fixes the problem in the debugging plan, where the only individual checkpoint landed at minute 46 and told me nothing I could still act on. A habit turned out to be doing structural work, and it is worth keeping on purpose now that I can say why.

The overlay is what stops the demonstration blocks from being passive. While I write and trace a loop, students are committing to a prediction in a sentence stem; while I break a program on purpose, they are filling guided notes. Neither block asks them only to watch.

What I am less sure about is that both bookends are the same timed, multiple choice format. It is what makes them comparable, so I would not give that up lightly. But a student who freezes under a countdown is invisible to me at minute five and invisible again at minute forty five, and that is the student this plan is least likely to catch.

## How this shapes my teaching

Budgeting the minutes first worked, and it is the habit I want to carry into every session plan. Hands on the keyboard is the number I care about, and deciding it up front is what made it the largest share here.

I am keeping the overlay structure, because it turns the thing I am most naturally drawn to, live demonstration, into something with a task attached. My [Lecture Style Inventory](/teaching/lecture-style-inventory/) result says demonstration is where my instincts go and that the Kahoot is functioning as a measurement instrument for me rather than as a source of energy. This plan is that result drawn out: a session built on demonstration, with checks on either side of it to find out whether the demonstration landed.

The change I would make before running it is to the exit check. Keeping the opening Kahoot for the comparison, and closing on something untimed and written, would still tell me what the class learned without asking a student to prove it against a countdown.
