## What the activity asked

Rate forty statements about how I run a classroom, from strongly disagree to strongly agree, and receive a score on each of five teaching styles: Expert, Formal Authority, Personal Model, Facilitator and Delegator.

## What I made

## What I learned

Facilitator came out highest, and that style is defined by the personal nature of teacher and student interactions. Three weeks later the [Instructor Motivation Quiz](/teaching/instructor-motivation/) scored me zero on Connection. I prefer the pedagogy of connection without being motivated by connection itself, which means the thing I rate highest is the thing I am least likely to keep doing when a term gets busy.

The warning is in the Personal Model description rather than in my score. Personal Model is my second highest, and the inventory's own stated disadvantage for it is that students who deviate from the instructor's preferred approach may feel inadequate. Debugging, which is the topic I plan most carefully, is a skill with many valid routes through it.

## How this shapes my teaching

The Personal Model risk has a concrete guard already written into my [debugging lesson plan](/teaching/debugging-lesson-plan/): a debrief question that asks the class to compare different approaches to the same broken program. That is also the block I was about to shorten for time. It is the one block that protects students whose process does not look like mine, so it stays.

Facilitator being my top style and Connection being my lowest motivator tells me to schedule relationship building structurally, through held office hours and a check-in rhythm, rather than trusting that I will do it because I believe in it.
