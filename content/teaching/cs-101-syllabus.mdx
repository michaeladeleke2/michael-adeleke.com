## What the activity asked

Build a complete syllabus for a hypothetical introductory course: description, prerequisites, student learning outcomes, required materials, a topic outline, assessment weights, a grading scale, policies for missed and late work, an attendance and participation policy, and a week by week schedule.

## What I made

CS 101: Introduction to Computer Science, three credits, taught in Python with no required textbook and all materials delivered through the LMS. I wrote eight student learning outcomes covering explanation of core concepts, implementation, decomposition, debugging, code reading and prediction, communication, collaboration, and self reflection. Assessment is split across eight categories: weekly programming activities 15%, debugging activities 10%, homework 15%, quizzes 10%, midterm 15%, final project 20%, final reflection 5%, and participation 10%. The schedule runs seven units across sixteen weeks, ending with project presentations and a final reflection.

## What I learned

Two policies in this syllabus contradict each other, and I did not notice until I reread it. The grading section says grades are based on demonstrated learning rather than completion, and the late work section says there is no penalty for correcting mistakes during designated revision opportunities. A few lines later, late work carries a 10% deduction per day for up to one week, which means an assignment turned in a week late is capped at 30% no matter how good it is. That is a completion penalty sitting inside a syllabus that says it does not use completion penalties. The choice I would defend is giving debugging its own 10% line rather than folding it into programming activities, because it makes the debugging outcome something I have to grade rather than something I assume happens. The weak spot is the reflection outcome, which rests entirely on a 5% final reflection and is never assessed until the last week of the term.

## How this shapes my teaching

The first fix is the late policy. I would replace the daily escalation with a flat cap, something like one week late at a fixed 20% deduction, so the penalty does not swallow the revision philosophy I wrote two paragraphs earlier. The second is to build an outcome to assessment map before I build the schedule, so I can see which of the eight outcomes have a graded home and which are decorative. Right now the reflection outcome has one, and it arrives too late for anyone to act on. I would keep debugging as its own line, and I would add the participation rubric this syllabus implies but never provides, since I promised students that participation can happen through written responses, collaborative programming, or demonstrations without telling them how any of those are judged.
