## What the activity asked

Ten forced-choice items about what would make me feel affirmed, satisfied and influential as a teacher. Each is worth five points, and the fifty points distribute across five motivators.

## What I made

## What I learned

Every available point landed in two of the five categories. Because the items are forced choice, a zero does not mean the motivator is absent, it means I never once chose it over the alternative on offer. Across ten chances, Connection never won.

That is the result worth sitting with, because the [Engaged Teaching Inventory](/teaching/engaged-teaching-inventory/) five weeks earlier put Facilitator at the top of my teaching styles, and Facilitator is the style built on teacher and student interaction. I am drawn to a way of teaching that runs on something I am not personally motivated by.

## How this shapes my teaching

Influence and Enlightenment at thirty and twenty is a teacher who wants students to leave thinking differently. That is a real motivation and it will keep me preparing material carefully. It will not, on its own, get me to office hours in week nine.

So the relationship work has to be structural rather than motivational: office hours that exist on the syllabus and are actually held, a reason for students to come to the first one before they need it, and a habit of contacting students whose grades drop rather than waiting for them to appear. My [CS 101 syllabus](/teaching/cs-101-syllabus/) promises that outreach. This is the result that tells me I will have to engineer it.
