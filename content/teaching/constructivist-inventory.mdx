## What the activity asked

Answer four questions about what learning is, what a teacher is for, what a learner's role is, and which activities I would choose, picking the statement closest to my own view. The answers map onto four theoretical frameworks.

## What I made

## What I learned

The activity is called You Might Be a Constructivist If, and the answer for me is mostly no. Cognitivism came out on top, which fits: I keep building lessons around sequence, worked structure and how much a novice can hold at once.

The honest reading of the top three, though, is that three against two against two is not a result. On seven responses, one different answer reorders the chart. What is worth reading is the zero. Humanism is the framework that centres the learner's own feelings and self-direction, and not one of my four answers pointed at it. That is the same blind spot that showed up in the [engagement cases](/teaching/engagement-cases/), where I marked a student saying "I'm just not a CS person" as a motivation problem rather than an engagement problem.

## How this shapes my teaching

Cognitivism as a default serves introductory CS well, and I am not trying to talk myself out of it. The useful instruction is the negative one: the affective side of a classroom is what I will underweight, and underweighting it is a pattern across these activities rather than a one-off. So it has to be built in deliberately. In CS 101 that means a check on how a student feels about the course that is separate from how they are scoring in it, early enough in the term to act on.
