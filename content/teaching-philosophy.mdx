---
title: "Teaching philosophy statement"
context: "Written for AHE 603, College and University Teaching"
---

I firmly believe that students learn most effectively when they encounter
real-world examples, can make meaningful connections, and actively engage in
the learning process by asking questions. For instance, in the field of
Computer Science, when teaching Python, I demonstrate how to identify syntax
errors and provide guidance on debugging them.

As an educator, my primary role is to guide, support, and provide assistance to
students whenever they require it. It is equally crucial for me to be vigilant
and observe the signs that indicate when students are not reaching their full
potential. By actively monitoring their grades and reaching out to students who
may be struggling in the class, I can identify the underlying reasons for their
difficulties and explore ways to offer them the necessary support.

One effective way to connect course content to students' prior knowledge or
experiences is by referencing information they should have learned in previous
classes. This can be achieved through pre-quizzes that help us identify any
gaps in understanding before delving deeper into the subject matter.
Additionally, I strive to make the content culturally relevant by incorporating
examples that resonate with students' backgrounds and experiences.

I believe that meaningful learning occurs when students actively engage in the
process, generating their own examples and working collaboratively to find
solutions. While quantitative indicators such as test scores and quiz scores
are important, I find that the most significant measure of student success is
their ability to identify problems, brainstorm potential solutions, and
actively work through the problem to reach a solution.

I am committed to continuous professional development by actively seeking
feedback from students and staying informed about the latest trends in
education. I recognize that the key to effective teaching lies in the teacher's
willingness to adapt and adjust their approach to meet the diverse needs of
students and ensure their long-term success.
